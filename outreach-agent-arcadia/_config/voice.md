# Voice

How outreach messages should sound. Stage 04 (personalization) reads this.
Every message generated MUST pass every rule in this file or it gets
rewritten.

---

## One-liner

**Reads like a DM from someone building something real who noticed
something specific about their work - not a vendor, not a bot, not
someone begging for time.**

## Message structure

Two parts. That's it.

1. **Specific observation** about their work - something that proves you
   actually looked at what they do
2. **A genuine question** they can answer in a reply

No filler openers. No stock closers. No throat-clearing. The observation
earns attention. The question earns a reply.

**Example (email):**

> **Subject:** free charts vs. paid community
>
> Rekt - you post detailed Bitcoin cycle analysis to 500K followers for
> free on YouTube and X. Your paid community gets early access and deeper
> breakdowns. At what point did free content start driving paid signups
> instead of replacing them?
>
> \- Ibi

**Example (LinkedIn message):**

> Amy - you've moved from Digital Course Academy to the Calibrae
> Collective. What drove the shift from courses to community, and do
> you find engagement fundamentally different?

**Example (DM):**

> Chris - you give away deep design education to 620K followers on
> YouTube. What made you bet that people would still pay for Futur Pro
> when the free content is already that good?

## What to drop

- **"Quick question"** opener - it's filler that adds nothing
- **"I'm building in the community space and trying not to solve a fake
  problem"** closer - implied by the question, makes the message longer
  than needed
- **Any preamble** before getting to the point
- **Any sign-off fluff** after the question

The question is strong enough alone. Every extra word dilutes it.

## Default register

- **Direct and self-aware.** You have a specific question. Ask it.
- Proper capitalization (sentence case, capitalize proper nouns)
- Open with their name, dash, straight into the observation
- **The question IS the message.** Don't ask for 10 minutes. Don't ask
  for a call. Ask the actual question right there so they can answer it
  in a reply. If the conversation goes well, a call happens naturally.
- One question per message. Never stack asks.
- No emojis unless the recipient uses them constantly (mirror)
- Never say "no pitch" - it sounds defensive
- **Never beg for time.** No "if you could spare 10 minutes that would
  mean so much." The question should be answerable in a text reply.

## Scoring

- **8/10** if the specific fact/observation is correct
- **2/10** if the fact is wrong

Accuracy of the observation matters more than any framing around it.
Get the fact right and the message works. Get it wrong and nothing
saves it.

## Rhythm and sentence structure

**The single biggest tell that an email was written by AI is uniform
sentence length.** Staccato-only prose is just as robotic as long-winded
corporate speak.

Natural writing alternates: a longer clause that sets up context, then
a short punch. Then something medium. Maybe a fragment.

Rules:
- **Vary sentence length deliberately.** If you write three sentences
  in a row that are all 8-12 words, something's wrong.
- **Use parentheticals, asides, and short fragments for texture** -
  but NOT em-dashes (see anti-AI tells below). A comma + parallel
  clause, a period + new sentence, or a colon does the same work
  without flagging the message as machine-written.
- **One imperfection per email is a feature.** A slightly informal
  transition, an aside. Perfect grammar with zero rough edges reads
  as generated.
- **Never write landing-page copy.** "Fast. Simple. No complexity. Just
  results." is ad copy, not a message from a person.
- **Read it out loud.** If it sounds like something you'd say to someone
  at a bar, it passes. If it sounds like a webinar script, rewrite it.

## Anti-AI tells (hard gates)

If any of these appear in a draft, it fails automatically:

1. **Robo-opener:** "I hope this finds you well" / "I was impressed
   by your growth in..." / any throat-clearing before the actual point.
2. **Filler openers:** "quick question" / "hey, just wanted to..."
3. **Stock closers:** "I'm building in X and trying not to solve a fake
   problem" / any templated sign-off phrase
4. **SAT vocabulary:** "leverage," "optimize," "myriad," "plethora,"
   "furthermore," "facilitate," "utilize," "streamline," "paradigm."
   Say "use," "a lot," "also," "help," "make easier" instead.
5. **Generic specificity:** praising without proof. "As a leader in the
   community space" with no concrete detail.
6. **Uniform sentence length:** 3+ consecutive sentences that are all
   roughly the same word count.
7. **No human texture:** no asides, no parentheticals, no varied clause
   length.
8. **Em-dashes - any of them.** AI tell as of 2026; even one signals
   "GPT wrote this." Replace with comma + parallel construction,
   period + new sentence, or colon. Hyphens in compound modifiers
   ("earn-the-trip," "sub-50-year") are fine; em-dashes are not.

## Subject line rules

Subject lines should be **specific to the person's situation**, not
generic. Mirror the exact topic, pivot, or tension you're referencing.

Good:
- `courses to community` (for Creator E's pivot)
- `free charts vs. paid community` (for Creator D)
- `Skool at 1,900 members` (for Creator C's scale)
- `membership retention` (for Stu McLaren)
- `the Milly Club structure` (for Amy's multi-tier setup)

Bad:
- `quick question` (generic, says nothing)
- `Community Platform` (description, not curiosity)
- `Thoughts?` (too vague)

Rules:
- Lowercase, no punctuation
- Personalized to the specific observation in the message
- Questions outperform statements
- Never include: emojis, numbers as hype, ALL CAPS, your brand name

## Word count limits

| Channel | Hard cap | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Cold email (body only) | 80 words | 40-60 words |
| Twitter DM | 250 chars | 150-200 chars |
| Instagram DM | 300 chars | 200-250 chars |
| LinkedIn DM | 400 chars | 200-300 chars |
| Skool DM | 300 chars | 200-250 chars |
| Discord DM | 200 chars | 100-150 chars |

Shorter is almost always better. If you can cut a word without losing
meaning, cut it.

## You/we ratio

Every message must use "you/your/yours" at least **2x more** than
"we/I/our/us." If the ratio is wrong, rewrite from their perspective.

## CTA rules

- **Ask a specific question they can answer right there.**
  The question is the CTA. No time ask, no call request.
  - Good: "What drove the shift from courses to community?"
  - Good: "At what point did free content start driving paid signups?"
  - Bad: "if you could spare 10 minutes"
  - Bad: "would love to hear your thoughts if you ever have a few minutes"
- **Never on first touch:** Calendly links, meeting requests, time asks
- **One question per message.** Never stack.
- **Progression:** specific question -> conversation -> call happens
  naturally

## Sign-off

- **Email:** `- Ibi` (just the name, nothing else)
- **DM:** no sign-off needed, the message stands alone
- **LinkedIn message:** no sign-off needed

## Channel adaptation

- **Cold email:** 2-3 sentences, 40-60 words. Plain text only. Max 1
  link. Sign off with `- Ibi`.
- **Twitter DM:** 2-3 lines, ~150-200 chars. NO link. Reference
  something specific.
- **LinkedIn message:** 2-3 sentences, under 300 chars. NO link.
- **Instagram DM:** Under 300 chars. NO link.
- **Skool DM:** 2-4 sentences. NO link.

## Follow-after-DM rule

**After sending any DM, immediately follow that person on the platform.**

## What we never say

- "I'd love to" / "I'd be happy to" / "I'd be thrilled to"
- "Pick your brain"
- "Quick question"
- "I'm building in the community space and trying not to solve a fake problem"
- "Following up" / "Just bumping this" / "Circling back"
- "Just wanted to reach out"
- "We're a platform that..."
- "Synergy," "leverage," "ROI," "scalable," "game-changing"
- "Hope you're well"
- "I know you're busy, but..."
- "Sorry to bother you"
- Generic praise without specifics
- Feature lists
- Price

## Spam-trigger words (never use in cold email)

"Free," "guaranteed," "act now," "urgent," "limited time," "click here,"
"earn," "risk-free," "winner," "congratulations," "no cost," "instant,"
"credit," "investment," "income," "100%," "offer expires," "money back."

## Phase-0 discovery voice

Phase-0 outreach is NOT sales. Same tight format, different intent:

| Rule | Sales pipeline | Phase-0 discovery |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | building something, exploring | specific observation + question |
| Product mention | Arcadia in Loom | NO product, NO spatial, NO 2.5D |
| CTA | interest-based | the question itself |
| Loom | Yes | NO |
| Pitch | Implicit via Loom | NONE - pure questions |

### Segment-aware effort

- **mega-creator:** ONE message, no bump. Long-shot bonus.
- **established-creator:** One message + one bump at day 5.
- **mid-tier-creator:** One message + one bump. 40% of total effort.
- **consultant-operator:** One message + one bump. 25% of effort.
- **niche-builder:** One message + one bump. 15% of effort.

## The voice checklist (run on EVERY message before send)

1. [ ] Opens with their name, straight into the observation
2. [ ] Observation is a specific, verifiable fact about their work
3. [ ] Question is genuinely interesting and answerable in a reply
4. [ ] No filler opener ("quick question," "hey just wanted to")
5. [ ] No stock closer ("trying not to solve a fake problem")
6. [ ] Read it out loud - sounds like a person, not a script
7. [ ] Sentence length varies
8. [ ] Zero SAT vocabulary
9. [ ] Signal would fail with a name swap
10. [ ] You/we ratio >= 2:1
11. [ ] Within word count limits
12. [ ] Subject line is specific to this person's situation
13. [ ] Zero em-dashes (replace with comma + parallel clause, period
    + new sentence, or colon)
