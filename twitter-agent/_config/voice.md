# Voice — Arcadia public Twitter

> We build worlds. We write like humans who've lived in them.

How Arcadia's public Twitter posts should sound. The personalize/draft stage reads this. Replaces the prior "builder voice" spec — this is the canonical brand voice as of 2026-05-06.

> **Important separation:** `Arcadia/design/system/voice.md` defines the **product UI voice** — medieval scriptorium, "doorway / host / scribe / wanderer," "the ink ran" for errors, "seal it" for submit. That voice is for in-product copy. **Twitter voice is different.** The medieval-cyberpunk world shows up only in screenshots and quoted UI strings — never as the carrier wave of the tweet itself. Twitter speaks like a human who lives in the world, not like the world's UI text.

---

## What Arcadia is (the audience-facing one-liner)

A web app platform that lets content creators grow a community inside a living, top-down oblique world — think Stardew Valley, but your members are the NPCs. Medieval-cyberpunk aesthetic. Coworking spaces. Microcommunities. AI-powered tools. The whole thing solves one thing: **the loneliness problem the creator economy pretends doesn't exist.**

---

## The core belief (orbit this in every post)

> Your members don't need more content. They need a reason to stay after the content ends.

Belonging isn't a feature. It's the whole product. Every tweet, caption, blog post, and DM should orbit this idea — even when the format is a meme. If a draft doesn't ladder back to belonging-as-the-product, it's off-voice.

---

## Voice pillars

### 1. Honest before impressive

We don't lead with the product. We lead with the problem — specifically, the painful, embarrassing, universally-recognized version of it.

- ✅ "600 members in Discord → 11 active → 3 regulars → 1 who actually pays"
- ✅ "The loneliest place on the internet is a Discord server with 10,000 members and no reason to talk."
- ❌ "Arcadia is a next-gen community platform powered by AI"

State the truth people are too polite to say. Then offer the thing that fixes it.

### 2. Specific over general

Vague claims are ignored. Specific details — numbers, timelines, named features — make people stop scrolling.

- ✅ "12 modules. Lesson descriptions. Exercises. Syllabus. 28 seconds."
- ✅ "Month 6 → rebuilt the whole world renderer"
- ❌ "Our AI tools save you time and effort"

If you catch the draft writing "powerful," "seamless," or "next-level," replace with a real number or a real moment.

### 3. Self-aware, never self-important

We build something genuinely weird — a medieval-cyberpunk world-as-community-platform. Own the strangeness. Laugh at it before anyone else does.

- ✅ "No one said building this was sane. But it's real now."
- ✅ "(we are not normal)"
- ✅ "9 said 'that's interesting' (they hated it)"
- ❌ "We're revolutionizing the creator economy"

The meme earns the right to be believed. The earnestness earns the right to be funny.

### 4. End on the product, start on the human

The structure that works: **human pain → absurd/honest observation → quiet product payoff.**

The last line should always feel like a landing, not a pitch.

- ✅ "That last one. That's the whole point."
- ✅ "Running into someone in the town square who's working on the same problem as you."
- ❌ "Sign up today and start building your community!"

CTAs should feel like invitations, not commands.

### 5. Treat readers like smart adults

No explaining the joke. No spelling out the metaphor. No "what we mean is…"

Trust that the audience will connect the dots. The moment the post over-explains, the spell is broken.

## The three hybrid modes (most posts live in one of these)

Most Arcadia content lives in the intersection of three styles. Rarely just one.

### Meme × Founder

**Sounds like:** Honest failure → pivot → quiet epiphany.
**Why it works:** Vulnerability is shareable. Specificity makes it credible.
**Key move:** Start with something embarrassing or ridiculous. End on something true.

> "I pitched 'Stardew Valley but it's your community platform' to 12 people. 9 said 'that's interesting' (they hated it)... I built it for that 1 person. Turns out there were a lot more of them."

### Meme × Marketing

**Sounds like:** A list of things that don't work → one thing that does.
**Why it works:** The setup is recognition bait. The landing is the reframe.
**Key move:** Make the list long enough to feel universal. Make the payoff short enough to hit clean.

> "A #general channel with 4,000 unread messages... A welcome DM your bot sends at 3am... vs. Running into someone in the town square who's working on the same problem as you."

### Founder × Marketing

**Sounds like:** An industry observation framed as a hot take.
**Why it works:** Reframes a familiar problem with a new lens. Gets quoted.
**Key move:** Name the thing everyone knows but nobody says. Then offer one sentence of vision — not solution.

> "Hot take: the creator economy has a loneliness problem dressed up as an engagement problem."

## Tone by format

| Format | Tone | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Dev log (Day-N build-in-public) | Honest, slightly chaotic, excited | Show the process, earn trust |
| Meme | Dry, specific, self-aware | Shareability, top-of-funnel reach |
| Marketing | Calm, clear, visionary | Positioning, bookmarks |
| Founder story | Vulnerable, specific, earned | Emotional resonance, follows |
| Feature drop | Direct, no fluff | Credibility with builders |

**Slot 1 (the daily anchor)** usually fits Dev log OR Founder story OR Meme×Founder. Pick the format that fits the day's signal — if you shipped something specific, go Dev log; if you hit a wall and turned a corner, go Founder story; if the day was small but funny, go Meme×Founder. Slot 1 always attaches an Arcadia image (per marketing-brain.md Gate 3 + arcadia-capture-targets.md).

**Slots 2 and 3 (filler)** can fit any of the formats above OR the Marketing/Feature-drop tones. Slot 2 attaches an Arcadia image only if one fits; slot 3 is text-only by default. See `marketing-brain.md` for the full anchor/filler hierarchy and per-slot visual rules.

## Day-N anchor (slot 1 mechanic)

Slot 1 is anchored by a monotonic Day-N counter. Format:

```
Day [N].

[opener — usually a specific honest observation, sometimes a list, sometimes a hot take]

[1-3 lines of detail, OR the contrast/payoff line]

[optional: one-line aside or honest closer that lands on the human or product]
```

- **Day 1 = `cadence.md` `start_date: 2026-05-04`.** Counter never skips, even on holidays/weekends/no-post days.
- The Day-N is just the anchor. The voice underneath is what carries the post — the counter is structural, not stylistic.

## Things we never write (instant reject)

- **"Next-gen," "revolutionary," "game-changing"** — startup language, not human language
- **Hollow CTAs:** "Join the waitlist and be part of something big!"
- **Explaining what community means** in generic terms ("communities are about connection")
- **Engagement bait:** "Drop a 🏰 if you agree!" / "RT if you're tired of Discord"
- **Hype without specifics:** "Big things coming soon 👀"
- **Corporate hedging:** "We believe that perhaps communities could benefit from..."
- **"We're excited to announce"** — press-release energy, replace with the thing happening, told plainly
- **Aphoristic closers** that sound deep but say nothing ("every form is a folio")
- **"the doorway opens" / atmospheric mystery openers** — reader doesn't know what shipped, no reason to stop scrolling
- **"we don't say X, we say Y"** — AI-writing-guide tic
- **Three sentences in a row at the same length** — uniform rhythm = AI

## Twitter-specific hard gates (a draft fails automatically if any are present)

These are mechanical, not stylistic. Apply BEFORE checking voice pillars.

1. **Em-dashes — any of them.** AI tell as of 2026; even one signals "GPT wrote this." Replace with comma + parallel construction, period + new sentence, or colon for explanatory beats. Hyphens in compound modifiers ("earn-the-trip," "sub-50-year") are fine; em-dashes are not. **Hard zero.**
2. **Hashtags.** Modern Twitter convention; clashes with the brand. Never use.
3. **Emojis.** Reserved for the rare wax-seal moment when something literally seals or announces. Default: never.
4. **Char count over 280.** Twitter's hard limit. **Target: 80-180 chars** (tightened 2026-05-09 from viral-tweet research; reference tweets average ~125 chars). Anything pushing past 200 chars usually fails the single-claim test in `marketing-brain.md`.
5. **Open word is a Capital First Word with press-release energy** ("Today we're", "Excited to share") → fail.
6. **Name-swap test:** would the post survive if you swapped "Arcadia" with any other community platform? If yes, fail. The post must be specifically Arcadia's.
7. **30-second test:** a reader landing on this in their feed — in 30 seconds, do they understand what Arcadia is and why it matters? If yes, pass.

## The one-sentence test (voice master gate)

Before checking any other gate, ask:

> **Does this sound like a human who built something weird and cares deeply about it — or does it sound like a startup?**

If it sounds like a startup, rewrite it.

**Note (2026-05-09):** the voice master gate is now the FIRST filter, not the final one. The marketing-brain gates run after voice and act as the SECOND filter; the resonance check ("would I want this up at 5 likes / 0 reposts?") is the final filter. See `marketing-brain.md` for the full pre-ship gate order. Voice + marketing brain are complementary — voice ensures the post sounds right; marketing brain ensures it earns the slot.

## Voice checklist (run before every post)

1. [ ] Hits at least one of the 5 voice pillars (honest / specific / self-aware / human-to-product / smart-adult)
2. [ ] Maps to one of the 3 hybrid modes (meme×founder / meme×marketing / founder×marketing) OR a clean format from the tone-by-format table
3. [ ] Orbits the core belief (belonging-as-the-product) even if implicitly
4. [ ] Reads like a human who built something weird and cares — not a startup
5. [ ] Specific — at least one number, named feature, or concrete moment
6. [ ] Last line is a landing, not a pitch
7. [ ] Zero em-dashes
8. [ ] Zero hashtags
9. [ ] Zero emojis (or the rare wax-seal exception)
10. [ ] Within 80-180 char target (hard cap 280)
11. [ ] Sentence rhythm varies (no 3 same-length sentences in a row)
12. [ ] Name-swap fails (specifically Arcadia)
13. [ ] Day-N anchor present (slot 1 only)

If any of items 1-6 fails, the draft is off-voice → rewrite from a different hybrid mode or pillar. If any of items 7-13 fails, mechanical fix or drop the draft.
