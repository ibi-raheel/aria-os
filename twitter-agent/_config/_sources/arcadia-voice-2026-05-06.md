# Arcadia — Brand Voice Guide

> We build worlds. We write like humans who've lived in them.

---

## What Arcadia is

A web app platform that lets content creators grow a community inside a living, top-down oblique world — think Stardew Valley, but your members are the NPCs. Medieval-cyberpunk aesthetic. Coworking spaces. Microcommunities. AI-powered tools. The whole thing solves one thing: the loneliness problem the creator economy pretends doesn't exist.

---

## The core belief (say this in every format)

> Your members don't need more content. They need a reason to stay after the content ends.

Belonging isn't a feature. It's the whole product. Every tweet, caption, blog post, and DM should orbit this idea, even when the format is a meme.

---

## Voice pillars

### 1. Honest before impressive

We don't lead with the product. We lead with the problem — specifically, the painful, embarrassing, universally-recognized version of it.

- ✅ "600 members in Discord → 11 active → 3 regulars → 1 who actually pays"
- ✅ "The loneliest place on the internet is a Discord server with 10,000 members and no reason to talk."
- ❌ "Arcadia is a next-gen community platform powered by AI"

State the truth people are too polite to say. Then offer the thing that fixes it.

---

### 2. Specific over general

Vague claims are ignored. Specific details — numbers, timelines, named features — make people stop scrolling.

- ✅ "12 modules. Lesson descriptions. Exercises. Syllabus. 28 seconds."
- ✅ "Month 6 → rebuilt the whole world renderer"
- ❌ "Our AI tools save you time and effort"

If you catch yourself writing "powerful," "seamless," or "next-level," replace it with a real number or a real moment.

---

### 3. Self-aware, never self-important

We build something genuinely weird — a medieval-cyberpunk world-as-community-platform. Own the strangeness. Laugh at it before anyone else does.

- ✅ "No one said building this was sane. But it's real now."
- ✅ "(we are not normal)"
- ✅ "9 said 'that's interesting' (they hated it)"
- ❌ "We're revolutionizing the creator economy"

The meme earns the right to be believed. The earnestness earns the right to be funny.

---

### 4. End on the product, start on the human

The structure that works: human pain → absurd/honest observation → quiet product payoff.

The last line should always feel like a landing, not a pitch.

- ✅ "That last one. That's the whole point."
- ✅ "Running into someone in the town square who's working on the same problem as you."
- ❌ "Sign up today and start building your community!"

CTAs should feel like invitations, not commands.

---

### 5. Treat readers like smart adults

No explaining the joke. No spelling out the metaphor. No "what we mean is…"

Trust that your audience will connect the dots. The moment you over-explain, you've broken the spell.

## The three hybrid modes

Most Arcadia content lives in the intersection of three styles. Rarely just one.

### Meme × Founder
**What it sounds like:** Honest failure → pivot → quiet epiphany  
**Why it works:** Vulnerability is shareable. Specificity makes it credible.  
**Key move:** Start with something embarrassing or ridiculous. End on something true.

> "I pitched 'Stardew Valley but it's your community platform' to 12 people. 9 said 'that's interesting' (they hated it)... I built it for that 1 person. Turns out there were a lot more of them."

---

### Meme × Marketing
**What it sounds like:** A list of things that don't work → one thing that does  
**Why it works:** The setup is recognition bait. The landing is the reframe.  
**Key move:** Make the list long enough to feel universal. Make the payoff short enough to hit clean.

> "A #general channel with 4,000 unread messages... A welcome DM your bot sends at 3am... vs. Running into someone in the town square who's working on the same problem as you."

---

### Founder × Marketing
**What it sounds like:** An industry observation framed as a hot take  
**Why it works:** Reframes a familiar problem with a new lens. Gets quoted.  
**Key move:** Name the thing everyone knows but nobody says. Then offer one sentence of vision — not solution.

> "Hot take: the creator economy has a loneliness problem dressed up as an engagement problem."

## Tone by format

| Format | Tone | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Dev log | Honest, slightly chaotic, excited | Show the process, earn trust |
| Meme | Dry, specific, self-aware | Shareability, top-of-funnel reach |
| Marketing | Calm, clear, visionary | Positioning, bookmarks |
| Founder story | Vulnerable, specific, earned | Emotional resonance, follows |
| Feature drop | Direct, no fluff | Credibility with builders |

---

## Things we never write

- "Next-gen," "revolutionary," "game-changing"
- Hollow CTAs: "Join the waitlist and be part of something big!"
- Explaining what community means in generic terms
- Engagement bait: "Drop a 🏰 if you agree!"
- Hype without specifics: "Big things coming soon 👀"
- Corporate hedging: "We believe that perhaps communities could benefit from..."

---

## The one-sentence test

Before posting anything, ask:

> Does this sound like a human who built something weird and cares deeply about it — or does it sound like a startup?

If it sounds like a startup, rewrite it.
